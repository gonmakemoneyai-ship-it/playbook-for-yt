## STRONG CONSENSUS
*(independently supported by 3+ of the 5 creators, ideally with platform-fact corroboration)*

**1. Packaging (title + thumbnail as one unit) is the single highest-leverage lever for the click/discovery stage, and gates everything downstream of it.**
- [Galloway] P1/P4: shift disproportionate effort from production to packaging; title+thumbnail treated as one complementary unit.
- [Eves] S1: "title and thumbnail... the most fundamental, most important step because if they don't click on it, YouTube's never going to promote it."
- [MrBeast/C&S] S3: A/B-tests thumbnails against a homepage-competition snapshot before publishing.
- [Hoyos] S1: the first frame of a Short explicitly functions as its thumbnail.
- [PLATFORM: Leung/Schmoyer] S1/S3: "a great video can still fail to get seen if the thumbnail is weak"; discoverability is the *first* thing checked when views are flat.
This is the most corroborated single idea across the entire research set, with direct platform-staff backing.

**2. CTR must never be read as a standalone success metric — it has to be read jointly with retention/satisfaction.**
- [Schmoyer] S5: explicit CTR × impressions × views quadrant model; a high-CTR/low-impression video is "niche appeal," not automatically a win.
- [Galloway] P18: retention swings that look small in percentage terms can be the difference between 100K and 1M views — implying CTR alone doesn't determine outcome.
- [Eves] S1/S2: CTR is the gate, but "session watch time" (what happens after the click) is what the algorithm is actually optimizing toward.
- [PLATFORM: Leung] S1: "tags mean nothing if your video is a letdown" — a direct platform-staff statement that post-click satisfaction, not pre-click metadata, ultimately governs outcomes.

**3. The opening of a video (first seconds to ~30 seconds) is the most consequential retention window and must actively earn continued viewing, not coast on the click already having happened.**
- [MrBeast/C&S] S5: direct, on-the-record mechanism — "On YouTube, at the start, you almost have to beg... you have to convince them of all of it and then get permission to slow down" — because viewers arrive skeptical that the title/thumbnail promise is real.
- [Galloway] P5: pre-production (idea→title→thumbnail→hook) is the highest-leverage phase; the Jon Youshaei interview structure gives the hook its own dedicated segment.
- [Hoyos] S1/S2: first-frame hook, "power words + foreshadowing" hook construction.
- [Schmoyer] S1 (uncertain attribution): an anecdote about top creators spending hours specifically on the first 30 seconds.

**4. Idea/topic selection sets a ceiling that packaging and execution cannot exceed — the idea matters more than production polish.**
- [Galloway] P2/P6: "the idea is more important than anything"; an idea has a TAM ceiling packaging can't exceed.
- [Eves] S3: "it's a data-centered human formula" — audience psychographics (who this is for) precede any tactic.
- [Hoyos] S1/S2/S5: a maintained bank of ~1,000 ideas; a 4-criteria idea-filter (novelty, uncertainty, knowledge gaps, complexity); outlier/trend detection as an ideation trigger.
- Partial tension with [MrBeast] S3's production-reinvestment philosophy — resolved below under Disagreements.

**5. Iterative, data-driven testing beats one-shot intuition, and this is a systematic habit, not a one-time exercise.**
- [Galloway] P9: "bangers and flops" review + 7-day post-mortem retention-curve analysis after every video.
- [Eves] S3: "Try → Fail → Analyze → Adjust"; the Squatty Potty case (165 title variants tested before launch, ~10x CTR uplift on the winner).
- [Hoyos] S4: readability-scores her own and competitors' scripts; a documented view-count crash-and-recovery after a deliberate operational change.
- [Schmoyer] S3: screenshot and compare retention graphs across multiple videos to spot recurring structural drop-off points.
- [MrBeast/C&S] S3: thumbnail/title A/B testing treated as a routine data practice.

**6. Treat the channel as a business; don't build the whole operation on ad revenue alone.**
- [Eves] S3/S5: explicit warning against ad-revenue dependency, "don't let YouTube be your only business."
- [Hoyos] S3/S4: monetization mix beyond AdSense (brand deals, consulting); "I'm a creator, but I'm an entrepreneur first."

## PARTIAL CONSENSUS
*(experts agree on the underlying goal but differ on execution/scale)*

**1. How much testing volume is actually necessary.** Eves's Squatty Potty case (165 title variants via paid traffic) is testing at an extreme, resourced scale; Galloway's "100-10-1" idea funnel is a lighter, ongoing ideation habit rather than paid A/B infrastructure; Hoyos's readability-scoring is a scripting-stage filter, not a packaging A/B test. All endorse *disciplined iteration over gut-feel*, but the concrete mechanism and resource requirement differ sharply by channel stage/budget — treat Eves's 165-variant example as the ceiling of what's possible with resources, not a baseline expectation.

**2. What exactly the "idea filter" should check.** Even within Hoyos's own five sources, the stated framework is not reconcilable into one canonical checklist (a 1,000-idea doc with multiple hooks per idea; "4 criteria" of novelty/uncertainty/knowledge-gaps/complexity; a 3-step passion/brand-fit/audience-resonance filter; a 2-step interest+feasibility-then-virality filter; outlier/trend detection). Galloway's Core/Casual/New audience-segment check and Eves's psychographics-first approach are two more, independently-arrived-at filters. **Read this as convergent evidence that structured idea-filtering matters a great deal, while the exact checklist is legitimately creator-specific** — the playbook's Idea Scoring System (below) synthesizes the common factors rather than adopting any single creator's exact list verbatim.

**3. How much to invest in Shorts vs. long-form.** Galloway's data study reports channels posting both grow subscribers roughly 2.8x faster than long-form-only channels [EXPERT OPINION, correlational, causation unconfirmed]. Colin & Samir self-report pushing into Shorts early, then deliberately pulling back to refocus on long-form. YouTube's own CEO, Neal Mohan, states the platform is deliberately format-agnostic and not trying to make either format "win" [PLATFORM FACT]. **Resolution: the platform doesn't force a tradeoff, but a given channel's audience/goals might make one format the better use of limited production time — this is a channel-fit decision, not a platform-imposed one.**

**4. When it's acceptable to sacrifice retention.** Galloway's Jon Youshaei interview has a segment titled exactly this, but its content could not be retrieved in this research pass — flagged as an open question the playbook cannot resolve with current sourcing. Treat "retention always wins" as the safe default until/unless better sourcing surfaces the specific exception cases Galloway has in mind.

## DISAGREEMENTS
*(respected sources point in different directions)*

**1. How much should a channel invest in production quality?**
- [MrBeast/C&S] S3: reinvests essentially all revenue into production quality (a $10M studio), explicitly treating production value as central to the strategy.
- [Galloway] P1: argues growing/smaller creators over-invest in production (~95% of effort) relative to its actual leverage on views, and should shift effort toward packaging instead.
- **Why they differ:** MrBeast's format *is* production value — stunts/spectacle are the content, so production spend is inseparable from the idea itself, and he has the capital and proven audience to justify it. Galloway is advising creators earlier in their growth curve, where packaging is almost always the more under-optimized, cheaper-to-fix lever, and production is rarely the binding constraint on a channel's next 10x. **Read as: production investment is a lever that pays off once the format demands it and the capital exists — for most growing channels, packaging is still the higher-leverage fix.**

**2. Is "long-form is dying due to shrinking attention spans" a real threat?**
- [Eves] S5: directly rebuts this — "humans, once they are curious, they're sucked into great storytelling" — but this claim is asserted without disclosed data in available material [flagged HYPOTHESIS, not proven].
- No other of the five sources directly contradicts this, but Colin & Samir's own reported Shorts-then-pullback-to-long-form anecdote and Hoyos's Shorts-first career are both consistent with a *both formats can work, format choice is about channel fit, not a global verdict on long-form's survival* reading rather than a clean agree/disagree split.

**3. Is aggressive reinvestment/scaling a reliably good strategy?**
- [MrBeast/C&S] S3 (2021): frames aggressive reinvestment as the source of his success.
- [MrBeast/C&S] S5 (2025): the same person, four years later, reports Beast Games (a major format-expansion bet) went over budget and was not "ROI positive... in terms of money."
- **This is the same creator's own account undercutting a simple "always reinvest everything, it always compounds" reading** — a genuine, self-reported caution against over-generalizing his earlier advice, not an outside critic's disagreement.

## OUTDATED ADVICE
*(may have worked historically, weak support for modern YouTube in this research)*

1. **Tags/keyword-stuffing as a primary growth lever.** Directly downgraded by a YouTube product executive [PLATFORM: Leung]: "tags mean nothing if your video is a letdown." No creator in this set treats metadata optimization as a top-tier lever; it is conspicuously absent from all five dossiers' extracted principles despite being one of the most commonly repeated pieces of beginner YouTube advice elsewhere.
2. **Treating "the algorithm" as a single system to be hacked.** [Eves] S2 explicitly names five distinct discovery surfaces (Browse, Suggested, Trending, Notifications, Search), each with a different objective; [PLATFORM: Mohan] frames long-form and Shorts as deliberately parallel, separate systems. Singular-"algorithm" framing (common in lower-quality guru content) does not match how any of these sources describe the platform.
3. **Precise posting-time optimization as a major growth lever.** Not mentioned as significant by any of the five sources' extracted principles. Consistent with the brief's own caution not to overstate this — the evidence gathered here provides no support for it as a meaningful lever at all, let alone a major one.

## MYTHS
*(common advice the evidence here does not support)*

1. **"More tags/keywords = more views."** Contradicted directly by platform staff [PLATFORM: Leung].
2. **"A viral hit guarantees the channel's future is set."** Both Hoyos's 160M-view Short (S5, sitting atop ~150 prior videos over ~2 years) and MrBeast's origin story (C&S S3, years of unmonetized iteration before breakout) frame outlier wins as the *product* of sustained iteration, not a lucky first try — undercutting the "one lucky video changes everything" narrative, and reinforcing that a channel needs a system, not a single swing.
3. **"Quality over quantity" as an unconditional rule.** [Hoyos] S4 explicitly reports rejecting this framing for "quality *and* quantity" after testing a deliberate quality-only slowdown and finding it cost real growth (a measured view-count crash) before a volume-inclusive approach recovered and exceeded prior performance.
4. **"Doing Shorts hurts your long-form channel" (or vice versa).** Directly contradicted by [PLATFORM: Mohan]'s stated format-agnostic platform design and by [Galloway]'s cross-posting growth-rate data.

## HIGH-CONFIDENCE PRINCIPLES
*(treat as core operating rules — corroborated across multiple creators AND at least one platform-fact-tier source)*

1. Packaging (title+thumbnail as a unit) gates all downstream distribution. *(Consensus #1 above)*
2. CTR and retention/satisfaction must be read together, never CTR alone. *(Consensus #2)*
3. The opening seconds must actively re-earn the click, not coast on it. *(Consensus #3)*
4. Idea/topic selection sets the ceiling; packaging and execution can only get you closer to it, never past it. *(Consensus #4)*
5. Systematic iteration (structured post-mortems, testing, comparison across videos) compounds results faster than isolated one-off improvements. *(Consensus #5)*
6. YouTube's distribution is multiple distinct surfaces/systems with different objectives, not one monolithic algorithm. *(Outdated Advice #2, stated by YouTube's own CEO)*

## CONTEXT-DEPENDENT PRINCIPLES
*(don't apply uniformly — depend on channel size, niche, format, maturity, or traffic source)*

- Optimal production-investment level (channel format/capital-dependent — MrBeast vs. a growing creator).
- Shorts vs. long-form investment ratio (audience/goal-dependent; platform itself is neutral).
- "Good" CTR benchmark — [Schmoyer] S5 explicitly states 2–10% depending on niche and traffic source; refuses a single number by design.
- Exact idea-filtering checklist (creator/niche-specific; multiple valid formulations exist even within one creator's own body of work).
- Growth-roadmap staging (Galloway's Establishment→Improvement→Optimization phases are explicitly for brand-new channels only, not applicable once a channel has track-record data).
- Whether to sacrifice retention for another goal (open question — see Partial Consensus #4).
